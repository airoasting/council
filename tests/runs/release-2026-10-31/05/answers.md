### 일론 머스크 · 라운드 2
Weakest link in the majority position: it assumes packaging works, and nobody has shown that. All three of us bet that the cost is the process and not the client. That is a guess. It could also be that a client paying under $5k expects attention out of proportion to what they pay, and that is a feature of the client, not of your intake form. A fixed-scope package doesn't stop a client from emailing at 9pm asking for "one small change." The enforcement falls on your account managers, and that is the same time sink under a new name.

The strongest case for Stop under $5k:

1. Time is the scarce input, and small clients spend the most of it per dollar. With 12 people, the account management hours these clients eat are hours not spent on the bigger clients you want. Your pipeline is thin partly because your best people are busy babysitting small work. If so, the small clients are the cause of the thin pipeline, and keeping them keeps the problem.

2. Packaging is adding a product line, not deleting. You'd build packages, intake forms, pricing pages, and rules, then police them. I ask first what we can delete. Stopping deletes a whole class of work with one decision. Packaging asks a stretched team to design and run a new system during a quarter when it has no slack.

3. A forced stop is a diagnostic. If the big pipeline stays thin once the small work is gone and the freed hours are pointed at outbound sales for larger clients, you learn what was really blocking growth. If you keep the small clients, you never find out.

4. The payroll risk is real but it is a number, not a feeling. It depends on what share of revenue the small projects bring in, and none of us knows that. If the share is well below 40% of revenue, because small projects are 40% of project count and not of revenue, then stopping costs much less than it sounds like.

The case holds if these are confirmed:
- Small clients are a clearly smaller share of revenue than of project count, and the account management hours they use are a large share of the total. I don't have a threshold from you, and I won't invent one.
- Cash runway covers at least the quarter it takes to redirect those hours into sales, with the payroll hit survivable even if the pipeline stays thin.
- Few small clients have grown into bigger ones. If they never do, they aren't a funnel.
- Your team has no real capacity to build and run packages right now.

Where it breaks: if the runway is short or the small revenue share is large, stopping can force layoffs, and that is hard to undo. In that case I would not stop.

Did arguing this change my position? Partly. I still say Package small work, but I now treat the packaging as a one-quarter test with an exit, so if the hours don't drop sharply, I stop.
